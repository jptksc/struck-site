It’s real ink — drag across a line to strike it, drag backward to take it off. The last row takes writing: try Call mom >friday !!

Done is a gesture, not a checkbox

Drag across a line and the ink lays down as far as you’ve crossed — a nib-width mark with a hand’s wobble in it, and past a point it finishes the stroke for you. The line then rests at 40% ink with the mark through it, exactly where you drew it, sitting among the live lines rather than dropping into an archive you’ll never open. Drag back to take it off.

Two keys are the whole interface

No Dock icon, no window to manage, nothing to launch. ⌥Space gives you one line to write on from inside any app — type, hit return, it’s gone. ⌥⇧Space opens the full page, exactly where you left it.

Type the details, don’t click them

A date, an hour, a repeat, a priority and a filing all fit inside the sentence you were already writing. Tokens dim as Struck recognises them; press return and they disappear, leaving small marks in the margin. Anything it can’t read stays in your title at full ink — nothing is silently dropped.

Nothing ever turns red

No badges, no counts, no progress rings, and no overdue state, anywhere. A task you keep carrying simply presses a little harder into the page each day. Age shows up as ink, not as an alarm.

One card at a time

An Inbox for whatever’s unfiled, Upcoming for everything with a date, and a card for each area of your life. Only one is on screen, ever. ⌘1–9 deals a card straight to the top; ⌘← and ⌘→ walk the deck.

Every line has a back

Press ⌘’ and the card turns over onto the same ruled paper. Write as long as you like; clip images and video against a line and they fan out in the margin like prints on a desk. There is no save button, because there is no way to lose one.

Paper you mix yourself

A card is a pane of tinted glass over your desktop, and its ink is derived from its paper so it stays legible over any wallpaper you own. Four levers — saturation, colour, tone, highlighter — mix the stock, and any card can wear its own.

It never leaves your Mac

Everything you write lives in one folder on your Mac — a plain SQLite file with whatever you’ve clipped sitting beside it, yours to copy or back up like any other. No account, no sign-in, no server of ours, nothing uploaded. It works offline because it has never been anywhere else. The only thing Struck itself ever sends anywhere is the open-or-closed state of an issue on a card you deliberately linked to GitHub — and if you switch on agent access below, whatever your assistant reads goes wherever that assistant runs.

$19.99, once

Free for two weeks, then a single purchase in the Mac App Store. No subscription, no tiers, no account to cancel. And when a trial ends Struck doesn’t lock you out of your own notebook — everything already written stays there to read, strike, edit and rearrange. What runs dry is writing new lines and making new cards.

A card can watch a repo

Point a card at a GitHub repository and its open issues arrive as lines on it. Strike one and the issue closes; close one on GitHub and it’s already struck here next time you look. Optional, off until you set it up, and it runs on a token you make yourself.

Let an agent write on it

Struck speaks MCP, so Claude — or anything else that speaks it — can read and write your cards in exactly the grammar you type. Off until you turn it on. It can capture, file, strike, unstrike and annotate, and it cannot delete a line, a card or a list — there is no delete verb to give it. An agent’s marks are ordinary marks: you strike, unstrike or rewrite them exactly as you would your own.
